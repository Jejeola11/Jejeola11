🩸 SEASON 2 — Red Gold Packed Cell Volume (PCV)

Episode 2.1 — What & Why (Aim)

Spin blood fast enough and it splits into layers like oil and water. The red layer at the bottom tells a doctor a LOT.

AIM: To determine the haematocrit — i.e. measure the percentage volume of red blood cells (RBCs) in whole blood after centrifugation. (This is past-paper Q4 — memorize word-for-word.)

PCV (a.k.a. haematocrit) = how much of your blood, by volume, is red cells.

Episode 2.2 — The Procedure (Microhaematocrit method) ⭐⭐

  1. Clean the prick site (ring finger or thumb) with 70% alcohol; let it dry.
  2. Prick with a sterile lancet to get a drop of blood.
  3. Hold the heparinised capillary tube at a slight angle; touch the red-ring end to the blood — it fills by capillary action.
  4. Fill the tube to about ¾ full; wipe excess blood off the outside.
  5. Seal the dry (non-blood) end by pressing into plasticine 3–4 times.
  6. Place in the micro-haematocrit centrifuge with the sealed end facing OUTWARD.
  7. Centrifuge at 3500 RPM for 10 minutes.
  8. Read with the micro-haematocrit reader (or measure columns with a ruler).
⚠️ Two methods to determine PCV (past-paper Q1 fill-gap): ① Microhaematocrit method (capillary tube + micro-centrifuge) and ② Macrohaematocrit / Wintrobe method (Wintrobe tube).

Episode 2.4 — The Formula, Normal Values & Clinical Importance ⭐⭐⭐

FORMULA:
PCV (%) = (Length of packed RBCs ÷ Total length of blood column) × 100
NORMAL PCV: Male = 40–54% · Female = 36–48%
Low PCV → anaemia, blood loss, nutritional deficiency.
High PCV → dehydration, polycythaemia.

4 Clinical Importance of PCV (past-paper-style "mention four"):

  1. Diagnosis of anaemia — low PCV signals reduced RBCs (e.g. iron-deficiency anaemia, blood loss).
  2. Detection of polycythaemia — high PCV suggests excess RBCs.
  3. Evaluation of dehydration / fluid status — PCV rises in dehydration (plasma drops, RBC number stays same).
  4. Monitoring treatment & disease progression — tracks response in anaemia, bleeding disorders, chronic illness, transfusion.
🧠 Normal PCV — "Men 40–54, Women 36–48": Women run a bit lower (menstrual loss). Same pattern as Hb!

🅰️ SEASON 3 — Type Casting Blood Group (ABO + Rh)

Episode 3.1 — Aim & The Reagents

AIM: To determine the ABO blood group (A, B, AB, or O) and the Rh factor (positive or negative) of a given blood sample.

Reagents (anti-sera) and their colour code:

Anti-serumColourDetects antigen
Anti-ABlueA antigen
Anti-BYellowB antigen
Anti-DColourlessD antigen (Rhesus)
Past-paper gaps: "Anti-A and Anti-B reagents determine the ABO blood group" (Q2). "Anti-D (anti-Rh) serum determines the Rhesus factor" (Q5).

Episode 3.2 — The Procedure

  1. Clean the white tile; label/partition 3 areas: Anti-A, Anti-B, Anti-D.
  2. Clean finger, apply pressure, prick with sterile lancet for 2–3 drops of blood.
  3. Place a drop of blood in each of the 3 marked areas.
  4. Add Anti-A to the 1st, Anti-B to the 2nd, Anti-D to the 3rd drop.
  5. Mix each with a separate stirrer/toothpick (never reuse — avoids false results).
  6. Time for 5 minutes, then observe for agglutination (clumping).
Reading it: Agglutination (clumping) = POSITIVE (+) for that antigen. Smooth / no clumping = NEGATIVE (−). For Rh: clumping with Anti-D = Rh positive; no clumping = Rh negative.

Episode 3.3 — The Master Results Table ⭐⭐⭐

If you memorize ONE table for blood grouping, make it this. (+ = clumps, − = smooth)

🟤 SEASON 4 — The Colour Match Haemoglobin Estimation (Sahli's)

Episode 4.1 — Aim & Principle

AIM: To determine the haemoglobin concentration of a given blood sample. (Past-paper Q6.)

The trick: turn haemoglobin into a brown colour (acid haematin), then dilute until it matches a standard brown reference. The amount of water you add tells you the Hb level.

Principle (Sahli's / acid haematin method): Blood + dilute HCl → haemoglobin converts to acid haematin (brown). Dilute with distilled water until its colour matches the standard comparison box; read Hb in g/dl off the tube.

Episode 4.2 — Components of the Haemoglobinometer ⭐ ("List 4")

ComponentRole
Sahli's pipette (Hb pipette)Measures exactly 20 µL (0.02 ml) of blood
Graduated test tubeWhere the reaction happens; read Hb in g/dl
Comparison / standard colour boxThe reference brown colour to match
Glass stirring rodStirs / mixes the solution
DropperAdds distilled water drop-by-drop
Reagent bottleHolds the 0.1 N HCl
Q3 (list 4 components): pick any four → pipette, graduated test tube, comparison box, glass stirring rod (also dropper, reagent bottle).

Episode 4.3 — The Procedure

  1. Clean materials; freshly prepare 0.1 N HCl.
  2. Prick finger (after applying pressure) with a sterile lancet.
  3. Suck blood up to the 20 µL mark using the Sahli's pipette.
  4. Disperse blood into the 0.1 N HCl; stir with the glass rod.
  5. Set aside 5 minutes → complete haemolysis & acid-haematin formation (brown colour).
  6. Add distilled water drop-by-drop, stirring, until colour matches the standard box.
  7. Read the haemoglobin value (g/dl) from the tube's graduation.
NORMAL Hb: Male = 13–18 g/dl · Female = 12–16 g/dl.
🧠 Remember the reagent: "Sahli's needs acid" → 0.1 N HCl makes acid haematin. Water only DILUTES to match the colour.

🫀 SEASON 5 — The Pressure Room Arterial Blood Pressure & Pulse

Episode 5.1 — Aim, Methods & Korotkoff Sounds ⭐⭐

AIM: To determine the arterial blood pressure of a subject.
Equipment: sphygmomanometer, stethoscope, stopwatch, subject.

Methods (all indirect / non-invasive):

MethodHowMeasures
PalpatoryFeel the radial/brachial pulse return on deflationSystolic ONLY
AuscultatoryListen with stethoscope for Korotkoff soundsSystolic AND Diastolic
OscillometricMachine senses arterial wall oscillationsSystolic, Diastolic & pulse
Korotkoff sounds: Phase 1 (first clear tapping) = SYSTOLIC BP. Phase 5 (last sound disappears) = DIASTOLIC BP.
⚠️ Past-paper theory (PSG 282 Q2): Auscultatory vs Palpatory.
Similarities: both indirect/non-invasive, both use the sphygmomanometer cuff, both occlude the brachial artery, both measure arterial BP.
Differences: Palpatory feels the pulse and gives only systolic (less accurate); Auscultatory uses a stethoscope to hear Korotkoff sounds and gives both systolic & diastolic (more accurate).

Episode 5.2 — The Procedure

  1. Subject sits comfortably, arm at heart level, relaxed, legs uncrossed; rest ~2 minutes.
  2. Wrap the cuff snugly around the upper arm, 2–3 cm above the antecubital fossa.
  3. Palpate the brachial artery; place the stethoscope over it.
  4. Inflate the cuff, then deflate slowly, listening.
  5. First clear tapping sound (Korotkoff phase 1) = Systolic BP.
  6. Last faint sound / disappearance (phase 5) = Diastolic BP. Record as Systolic/Diastolic mmHg.
NORMAL BP: ~120/80 mmHg (adult male). Adult female ≈ 110/70. Systolic >120, Diastolic >80 noted as raised.

Episode 5.3 — Pulse Rate

  • Pulse rate = number of heartbeats per minute, felt as the pulsation of an artery.
  • Where to feel it: Radial (wrist), Brachial (arm), Carotid (neck), Femoral (groin), Popliteal (knee).
  • Normal: Adult male 60–90 bpm; Adult female 70–90 bpm (women slightly higher at rest).
  • Factors affecting it: emotional stress/pain, medication, dehydration, caffeine, exercise, age.
  • Importance: assesses heart function, detects abnormalities, monitors response to exercise/treatment.
🧠 Pulse sites — "Really Big CatsFtft... R-B-C-F-P": Radial · Brachial · Carotid · Femoral · Popliteal. 🐱

Episode 6.1 — Anticoagulants (with examples) ⭐⭐ THEORY FAVOURITE

An anticoagulant is a substance that prevents blood from clotting. Examiners want examples + how each works + what it's used for.

AnticoagulantHow it worksUsed for (tube)
EDTAChelates (binds) calciumFull Blood Count / haematology (lavender top)
HeparinActivates antithrombin III (inhibits thrombin)PCV, blood gases, osmotic fragility (green top)
Sodium citrateChelates calciumCoagulation studies (PT/APTT) & ESR (blue top)
Oxalate (Na/K oxalate)Chelates calciumGlucose (with fluoride), some chemistry
Sodium fluorideInhibits glycolysis (+ weak anticoag)Blood glucose (grey top)
Key idea: Calcium (Factor IV) is essential for clotting. Most anticoagulants work by removing/binding calcium (EDTA, citrate, oxalate); heparin is the exception — it boosts antithrombin instead.
🧠 "Every Healthy Cat Outran Fido": EDTA · Heparin · Citrate · Oxalate · Fluoride.

Episode 6.2 — The Haemocytometer (Neubauer chamber) ⭐⭐

PSG 282 asks: "List and discuss the components of Haemocytometer." It's the tool for manually counting blood cells.

ComponentRole
Counting chamber (Neubauer slide)Thick glass slide with an H-shaped groove forming 2 ruled platforms
Ruled grid (Improved Neubauer ruling)Etched squares: 4 corner squares = WBC count; central square (with 25 small squares) = RBC count
Cover slipSpecial thick coverslip; creates a known depth of 0.1 mm
RBC diluting pipetteHas a red bead; dilutes blood 1:200 for RBC counts
WBC diluting pipetteHas a white bead; dilutes blood 1:20 for WBC counts
Remember: chamber depth = 0.1 mm; the grid lets you count cells in a known volume and calculate cells per mm³ (per microlitre).

Episode 6.3 — Clinical Methods to Examine the Heart (IPPA) ⭐

PSG 282 Q1: "Discuss the clinical methods for examination of the human heart." Four classic steps, in order.

MethodWhat you do
InspectionLook — chest shape, visible apex beat, pulsations, cyanosis
PalpationFeel — apex beat (normally 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line), thrills, heaves
PercussionTap — outline the heart borders / cardiac dullness
AuscultationListen with stethoscope — heart sounds S1 ("lub") & S2 ("dub"), murmurs (mitral, tricuspid, aortic, pulmonary areas)
Also mention: measuring pulse & blood pressure, and investigations like ECG, echocardiography, chest X-ray.
🧠 "I Put People to Auscultate" → IPPA: Inspection · Palpation · Percussion · Auscultation.

Episode 6.4 — Lab Safety / Precautions with Blood ⭐ ("List 4")

Past-paper Q8: "List four precautionary measures when working on blood samples."

  1. Wear gloves (and lab coat) — treat ALL samples as potentially infectious.
  2. Use a sterile, disposable lancet — one per person, never shared.
  3. Clean the prick site with 70% alcohol and let it dry.
  4. Dispose of sharps/lancets in a sharps (biohazard) container; never recap needles.
  5. No mouth-pipetting; wash hands after; clean spills with disinfectant; label samples properly.

⚡ NIGHT-BEFORE CRAM SHEET

PCV: = haematocrit = % RBC volume after spinning. Microhaematocrit (capillary+centrifuge, 3500 RPM ×10 min) or Wintrobe method. 3 layers: plasma (top), buffy coat = WBC+platelets (middle), packed RBC (bottom). PCV% = (RBC column ÷ total column)×100. Normal: M 40–54%, F 36–48%. Low→anaemia/blood loss; High→dehydration/polycythaemia.

BLOOD GROUP: anti-A(blue), anti-B(yellow), anti-D(colourless) on white tile, 5 min, agglutination=+. Anti-A clumps→A; anti-B→B; both→AB; neither→O. Anti-D clumps→Rh+. AB=universal recipient, O=universal donor.

HAEMOGLOBIN (Sahli's): 20µL blood + 0.1N HCl → acid haematin (brown), wait 5 min, add distilled water till colour matches standard box, read g/dl. Normal: M 13–18, F 12–16 g/dl. Components: pipette, graduated tube, comparison box, glass rod, dropper.

BLOOD PRESSURE: sphygmomanometer + stethoscope. Methods: palpatory (systolic only), auscultatory (both, Korotkoff), oscillometric. K1=systolic, K5=diastolic. Cuff 2–3cm above antecubital fossa. Normal 120/80 (M), 110/70 (F).

ANTICOAGULANTS: EDTA (FBC), Heparin (PCV/gases), Citrate (clotting/ESR), Oxalate, Fluoride (glucose). Most bind calcium; heparin boosts antithrombin.

HAEMOCYTOMETER: Neubauer counting chamber (depth 0.1mm), ruled grid (corners=WBC, centre=RBC), coverslip, RBC pipette (red bead, 1:200), WBC pipette (white bead, 1:20).

HEART EXAM: Inspection, Palpation, Percussion, Auscultation (S1 "lub", S2 "dub") + pulse, BP, ECG.

MICROSCOPE PARTS: eyepiece, objective lens, nose piece, stage, condenser, iris diaphragm, coarse & fine focus, arm, base, light source. Carry: hand on ARM + under BASE.

SAFETY: gloves, sterile one-use lancet, 70% alcohol, sharps bin, no mouth-pipetting.
