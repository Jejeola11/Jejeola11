🌅 SEASON 1 — The Birth of Wisdom What is Philosophy?

Episode 1.1 — "Love at First Thought" (Meaning of Philosophy)

In ancient Greece, a man named Pythagoras refused to call himself "wise." Too proud. Instead he said: "I'm just a lover of wisdom." And a discipline was born...

Philosophy comes from two Greek words:

  • Philo = love
  • Sophia = wisdom

So Philosophy literally means "Love of Wisdom." A philosopher is therefore a "lover of wisdom."

Guaranteed exam line: Philosophy literally means Love of Wisdom. "Sophia" = Wisdom. A philosopher = a lover of wisdom.
🧠 Mnemonic: "PHILO the dog SOFIa" → Philo (love) + Sophia (wisdom). Picture a dog named Philo madly in love with a girl named Sophia. 🐶❤️

Episode 1.2 — "Born from Wonder" (The Nature of Philosophy)

Why is the sky blue? Why do we die? Why be good? A child asks these. So does a philosopher. The difference? The philosopher never stops asking — and demands reasons.

Philosophy is critical inquiry — the rational, questioning examination of fundamental beliefs. It doesn't accept things blindly; it asks for justification.

  • Philosophy begins with WONDER (the first step of philosophical inquiry).
  • It promotes critical reflection, independent & open-minded thinking.
  • It opposes dogmatism, blind acceptance, superstition, and prejudice.
  • It questions fundamental assumptions.
Exam traps: Philosophy is primarily concerned with critical inquiry (NOT wealth/agriculture/engineering). Philosophy begins with wonder (NOT wealth/religion/politics).
🧠 Mnemonic: Philosophy = the "W-W-C" subject → starts with Wonder, asks Why, demands Critical reasons.

🥋 SEASON 4 — The Logic Dojo Core Logic ⭐⭐

This is the HEART of the course. Expect the MOST questions here. Slow down and master every line.

Episode 4.1 — "Anatomy of an Argument"

In logic, an "argument" isn't a shouting match. It's a clean machine with parts.

  • Logic = the study of correct reasoning (also: the process/patterns of reasoning).
  • An argument = premises + a conclusion.
  • Premises = the supporting statements/reasons.
  • Conclusion = the final claim drawn from the premises.
  • Inference = a conclusion derived from premises (the mental "jump").
One-liner to memorize: "An argument is a set of premises offered to support a conclusion." Logic is the tool that checks if that support is good.

Episode 4.2 — "Top-Down vs Bottom-Up" (Deduction vs Induction) ⭐⭐

Two detectives. Sherlock uses DEDUCTION (general rule → certain conclusion). A scientist uses INDUCTION (many observations → probable rule).

⬇️ DEDUCTIVE
Moves GENERAL → SPECIFIC.
Aims at CERTAINTY.
Conclusion is necessary (guaranteed if premises true).
⬆️ INDUCTIVE
Moves SPECIFIC → GENERAL.
Aims at PROBABILITY.
Involves generalization.
🧠 Mnemonic: "Deduction = Descends (general comes down to specific) = Definite/certain." "Induction = Increases (specific builds up to general) = In-probable... only probable."
⚠️ Most-missed fact: Deduction = CERTAINTY. Induction = PROBABILITY. Swap these and you lose easy marks.

Episode 4.3 — "Valid, Sound & True" (The Holy Trinity of Logic) ⭐⭐⭐

This trips EVERYONE up. Read it twice. A valid argument can still be nonsense — surprised? Let's fix that.

  • VALIDITY = the conclusion follows logically from the premises (good structure). Validity says NOTHING about whether premises are actually true!
  • So a valid argument may have FALSE premises. ✅ (Huge exam favourite.)
  • SOUNDNESS = Validity + all premises actually TRUE. (The gold standard.)
  • Invalidity = conclusion does NOT follow. Opposite of validity = invalidity.
The killer formula: SOUND = VALID + TRUE PREMISES. A deductive argument is strongest when it is valid AND sound.
🧠 Mnemonic: A SOUNDsystem needs the Speaker working (Valid structure) AND good music playing (True premises). Miss one → no good sound. 🔊
⚠️ Mind-bender (they ASK this): "An argument can be valid even if its premises are false." → TRUE. Validity is about FORM, not facts.

Episode 4.4 — "The 3 Unbreakable Laws" (Laws of Thought) ⭐⭐

Three laws so basic that without them, thinking itself is impossible. Break one and you talk nonsense.

LawStates that...Example
IdentityA is A (a thing is itself)A cat is a cat.
Non-ContradictionA cannot be A and not-A at the same timeYou can't be alive and dead simultaneously.
Excluded MiddleEvery statement is either true or false (no middle)"It is raining OR it is not raining."
Test links: "Either it is raining or it is not raining" = Law of Excluded Middle. An inconsistent/contradictory statement violates the Law of Non-Contradiction. "A is A" = Law of Identity.
🧠 Mnemonic — "I Never Eat": Identity · Non-contradiction · Excluded middle. 🍽️

🔐 SEASON 5 — The Syllogism Code Propositions & Symbolic Logic

Episode 5.1 — "The 3-Line Power Move" (Propositions & Syllogisms) ⭐

"All men are mortal. Socrates is a man. Therefore, Socrates is mortal." The most famous argument in history. It's called a SYLLOGISM.

  • Proposition = a statement capable of being true or false (NOT a question, command, or exclamation). Must be meaningful & truth-evaluable.
  • Syllogism = a deductive argument with exactly THREE propositions (2 premises + 1 conclusion).
  • Major premise = appears FIRST.
  • Middle term = the linking term; appears in both premises only (never in the conclusion). It links the major and minor terms.
  • Enthymeme = an incomplete syllogism (a premise is left unstated).
Worked example: "All men are mortal" (major premise, 1st) · "Socrates is a man" (minor premise) · "∴ Socrates is mortal" (conclusion). Middle term = "man/men" (it's in both premises, not the conclusion).
🧠 Mnemonic: Syllogism = "SILLY-gism has 3 lines" (like a silly 3-line poem). Middle term is the MIDDLEman who never shows up at the conclusion (the meeting). 🕴️

Episode 5.2 — "Symbol Warfare" (Symbolic Logic: Modus Ponens & Tollens) ⭐⭐

Logicians got tired of long sentences, so they replaced them with symbols (P, Q). Two attack patterns dominate.

  • Symbolic logic = uses symbols to represent statements.
PatternFormMeans
Modus PonensIf P then Q; P; ∴ QAffirming the antecedent ✅ valid
Modus TollensIf P then Q; not Q; ∴ not PDenying the consequent ✅ valid
In plain words:
• Modus Ponens = "Put it forward / affirm." If P→Q and P is true, then Q is true.
• Modus Tollens = "Take it away / deny." If P→Q and Q is false, then P is false.
🧠 Mnemonic: "Ponens = Positive = Puts forward the antecedent (P)." "Tollens = Takes away = denies (the consequent)." Ponens=P, Tollens=Takes. ✊

Episode 5.3 — "Always & Never" (Tautology, Contradiction, etc.)

TermMeaning
TautologyA statement that is ALWAYS TRUE (e.g. "It is raining or it is not raining").
ContradictionA statement that is both true and false at once (always false).
Necessary conditionMust exist for something else to occur.
🧠 Mnemonic: "Tautology = Totally True, always." "Contradiction = Con (against itself)."

Episode 5.4 — "The Nine Commandments" (First 9 Rules of Inference) ⭐ SYLLABUS-LISTED

Your official course outline literally says: "symbolic logic — the first nine rules of inference." So this is fair game. Don't memorize the symbols stiffly — just recognize each pattern by NAME. Symbols: ⊃=if/then, ·=and, ∨=or, ~=not, ∴=therefore.

#RulePatternPlain English
1Modus Ponens (MP)p⊃q, p ∴ qAffirm the "if" part → get the "then" part
2Modus Tollens (MT)p⊃q, ~q ∴ ~pDeny the "then" part → deny the "if" part
3Hypothetical Syllogism (HS)p⊃q, q⊃r ∴ p⊃rChain of "ifs" (p→q→r, so p→r)
4Disjunctive Syllogism (DS)p∨q, ~p ∴ q"Either/or" + one is false → the other is true
5Constructive Dilemma (CD)(p⊃q)·(r⊃s), p∨r ∴ q∨sTwo "ifs" + either antecedent → either consequent
6Simplification (Simp)p·q ∴ p"A and B" is true → so A alone is true
7Conjunction (Conj)p, q ∴ p·qA is true, B is true → "A and B" is true
8Addition (Add)p ∴ p∨qA is true → "A or anything" is true
9Absorption (Abs)p⊃q ∴ p⊃(p·q)If p→q, then p→(p and q)
🧠 Mnemonic for all 9 — "MM HD C SCA A":
Modus Ponens · Modus Tollens · Hypothetical Syllogism · Disjunctive Syllogism · Constructive Dilemma · Simplification · Conjunction · Addition · Absorption.
Try: "My Mum Has Done Cooking, So Come And Africa Absorbs" — or make your own silly sentence; the goal is just to recall the 9 names.
Exam reality: Most CBT questions only test the famous two (MP & MT) plus recognizing names like Hypothetical/Disjunctive Syllogism. Know all nine by name, master MP & MT cold.

Episode 5.7 — "The A-E-I-O Code" (Categorical Propositions) 📖 ⭐⭐ CLASSIC

Every "All/No/Some" statement fits one of FOUR types, labelled A, E, I, O. This is a guaranteed question.

TypeNameFormExample
AUniversal AffirmativeAll S are PAll men are mortal
EUniversal NegativeNo S is PNo man is a stone
IParticular AffirmativeSome S are PSome men are wise
OParticular NegativeSome S are not PSome men are not wise
⚠️ Manual typo alert: Your manual mislabels E as "particular affirmative." That's a printing error. The correct, standard answer is: A = Universal Affirmative, E = Universal Negative, I = Particular Affirmative, O = Particular Negative. Use these.
🧠 Mnemonic: A & I = Affirm (from Latin "AffIrmo"); E & O = deny/nego (from "nEgO"). Vowels of AffIrmo = A, I (yes); vowels of nEgO = E, O (no). Capital letters = universal, others particular.

Episode 5.8 — "Spotting the Parts" (Proposition Types & Indicators) 📖

  • Analytic / A priori proposition = true by definition, known by reason alone ("A bachelor is an unmarried man"; "A square has 4 sides"). Tautological.
  • Synthetic / A posteriori proposition = verified by sense experience ("It is raining"); common in the sciences.
Indicator words (how to spot premise vs conclusion):
  • Premise indicators: because, since, as, for, the reason that, in light of the fact…
  • Conclusion indicators: therefore, thus, consequently, as a result, hence, it follows that, I conclude…
3 types of deductive (syllogistic) arguments: Categorical (uses A/E/I/O), Disjunctive (either…or), Hypothetical (if…then). An assertion = a claim with NO supporting premise (not an argument).

⚡ THE 1-HOUR CRAM SHEET

No time? This is the ENTIRE course distilled. Read it 3×.

PHILOSOPHY = love of wisdom (Philo+Sophia). Begins with wonder. = critical inquiry. Philosopher = lover of wisdom.

5 BRANCHES: Metaphysics (reality/being) · Epistemology (knowledge) · Ethics (morality) · Logic (reasoning) · Aesthetics (beauty). Axiology = values. Ontology = being.

FATHERS: Socrates = father of Western Philosophy (Socratic method, "know thyself", taught Plato). Aristotle = father of Logic (Lyceum, taught by Plato). Plato = Academy + Allegory of the Cave.

SCHOOLS: Rationalism=reason (Descartes, "I think therefore I am"). Empiricism=senses (Locke, Hume). Pragmatism=utility (William James). Idealism=reality is mental. Materialism=reality is physical. Skepticism=doubts knowledge (opp. of dogmatism). Existentialism=human existence/freedom (Sartre).

LOGIC: = study of correct reasoning. Argument = premises + conclusion. Inference = conclusion from premises.
• Deduction: general→specific, CERTAINTY. Induction: specific→general, PROBABILITY.
• Valid = conclusion follows (can have false premises!). Sound = valid + true premises. Opposite of validity = invalidity.
• 3 Laws of Thought: Identity (A is A) · Non-contradiction (not A & not-A together) · Excluded middle (true or false).

SYLLOGISM = 3 propositions. Major premise = first. Middle term = in both premises, not conclusion; links major & minor. Enthymeme = incomplete syllogism. Proposition = statement that's true or false.

SYMBOLIC LOGIC = symbols for statements. Modus Ponens = affirm antecedent (P→Q, P, ∴Q). Modus Tollens = deny consequent (P→Q, ¬Q, ∴¬P).

FALLACY = error in reasoning. Formal=structure, Informal=content. Ad Hominem=person · Ad Verecundiam=authority · Ad Misericordiam=pity · Ad Baculum=force · Ad Populum=people. Straw man=distort opponent. Hasty generalization=too little evidence. Begging the question=circular. False dilemma=only 2 options.

TRUTH THEORIES: Correspondence=facts, Coherence=consistency, Pragmatic=utility. Knowledge = Justified True Belief.

ETHICS: Utilitarianism=consequences. Kant=duty. GOAL OF GST 212 = develop critical & logical reasoning skills.

NATURE OF PHILOSOPHY (3): Criticality, Reconstruction of ideas, Formulation of worldviews. Worldview tests: factual adequacy, logical coherence, explanatory capacity. Scope: first-order (the 4 core branches) vs second-order (philosophy of X). Kant = "queen of the sciences."

4 METHODS: Analytic (break down), Speculative (whole/unity), Prescriptive (prescribe solutions), Phenomenological (lived experience; Husserl; bracketing/epoché).

MORE ISMS: Theism=affirms God; Atheism=denies God; Realism=objects exist independent of mind (Aristotle); Idealism=reality is ideas (Plato); Monism=one reality; Dualism=mind+body; Materialism=matter (Epicurus). Berkeley="esse est percipi"; Locke="tabula rasa".

LANGUAGE: Functions = Informative, Directive, Expressive. Types = Private, Public, Ordinary, Ideal, Artificial.

5 CONNECTIVES: Conjunction · (and), Disjunction v (or), Conditional ⊃ (if-then), Negation ~ (not), Biconditional ≡ (iff). Variables = lowercase p,q,r; Constants = UPPERCASE.

CATEGORICAL PROPOSITIONS: A=Universal Affirmative (All S are P), E=Universal Negative (No S is P), I=Particular Affirmative (Some S are P), O=Particular Negative (Some S are not P). Analytic=true by reason; Synthetic=true by experience. Premise indicators: because/since/for. Conclusion indicators: therefore/thus/hence. 3 deductive syllogisms: Categorical, Disjunctive, Hypothetical.

FALLACY FAMILIES (informal): ① Ambiguity (Equivocation, Amphiboly, Accent, Composition, Division) ② Relevance (ad Baculum/force, ad Hominem/person, ad Verecundiam/authority, ad Misericordiam/pity, ad Populum/people, ad Ignorantiam/ignorance, Ignoratio Elenchi/missing point) ③ Presumption (False cause, Accident, Hasty generalization, Begging the question, Complex question). Formal fallacies: Undistributed Middle, Illicit Major/Minor, Existential.
