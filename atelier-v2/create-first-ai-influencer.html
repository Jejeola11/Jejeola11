Create Your First AI Influencer & Video Inside Fuse Atelier

You’ve probably seen a character appear in different outfits, join a familiar trend, or feature in a product video—and then discovered that the person was created with AI.

The interesting part is more than how realistic the character looks. It is how the creator brings the same recognizable character into different situations.

In this tutorial, you’ll learn how to do that inside Fuse Atelier.

We’ll start by creating your character in AI Influencer Studio, save a character sheet, then use Scene Remix to put that character into a video.

By the end, you should have:

  • A clear idea for your AI influencer.
  • A character sheet you can reuse.
  • Your first Scene Remix video.
  • A starting plan for publishing on Instagram and TikTok.

The tutorial is free to read. Image and video generation inside Fuse Atelier use credits. Check the displayed credit cost before generating.

1. Understand what you’re building

An AI influencer is a fictional character whose appearance and content are created using AI tools.

You decide what they look like, what they care about, how they communicate, and the kind of content they appear in.

For example, you could create:

  • A modest-fashion character who explores different outfits.
  • A beauty character who appears in skincare and makeup concepts.
  • A funny lifestyle character who finds herself in unexpected situations.
  • A food character who turns ordinary cooking moments into entertaining videos.
  • A virtual presenter for a business or educational page.

A realistic image gives you a starting point. A recognizable personality gives you something to build content around.

Ask yourself:

“Why would someone want to see this character again?”

Their style might be memorable. Their situations might be funny. Their content might be useful. Ideally, there is a clear reason to follow beyond their appearance.

2. Give your character an identity

Before generating, write a short character brief.

It does not need to be complicated. You are making a few decisions that will guide the images, videos and captions you create later.

Include:

Name: What will you call the character?

Age: Choose a clearly adult character for this tutorial.

Niche: What will their content mainly focus on?

Personality: Are they playful, elegant, confident, curious or dramatic?

Appearance: What facial features, skin tone, clothing style and accessories should remain recognizable?

Audience: Who are you creating the content for?

Content promise: What can someone expect when they follow?

Here is an example:

Name: Amina
Age: 25
Niche: Modest fashion and playful lifestyle content.
Personality: Confident, expressive and slightly dramatic.
Appearance: Deep brown skin, an oval face, brown eyes and a distinctive smile. Usually wears a neatly styled scarf and elegant, modest outfits.
Audience: Young adults who enjoy fashion, visual storytelling and relatable humour.
Content promise: Stylish outfits placed in entertaining, unexpected situations.

You can use a different name, background, gender, style or niche. The important thing is to make deliberate choices.

Keep this brief somewhere you can return to. It will help you make decisions when you are tempted to change everything after every generation.

3. Create your character in AI Influencer Studio

Open Fuse Atelier and go to Create, then open AI Influencer.

Choose the starting approach that suits your idea.

Build From Scratch

Use this when you want to create an original fictional character.

Work through the available appearance options. Choose the features that matter to your character, such as their skin tone, build, hairstyle or scarf, clothing style and distinctive details.

Start with a combination you can describe clearly. Too many competing ideas can make it harder to judge whether the result matches your intention.

For our example, we might choose:

A clearly adult Nigerian woman with deep brown skin, an oval face, expressive brown eyes, a neatly wrapped scarf and an elegant modest-fashion style.

Build From Me

Use this when you want to start from your own appearance.

Upload a clear reference photo where your face is easy to see. Avoid heavy filters, extreme camera angles or objects covering important facial features.

Use your own photo or a photo you have permission to use. For an original fictional influencer, build an original face rather than copying another creator’s identity.

Generate and review

Check the selected settings and displayed credit cost, then generate.

Treat the result as a first version you need to inspect. You do not have to accept an unsuitable character simply because the image looks polished.

Look for:

  • A face that suits your character brief.
  • Clear, readable facial features.
  • Clothing and accessories that make sense together.
  • Plausible hands, limbs and body proportions.
  • A close-up and full-body view that look like the same person.

If you want a revision, change something specific.

For example:

Keep the same face, skin tone and body proportions. Change only the scarf to light teal and the outfit to an ivory blouse with a long dark teal skirt. Preserve the character’s identity.

This gives the next generation a focused instruction.

4. Save your character sheet

Your character sheet is the reference you will use to bring your character back in future images and videos.

The face close-up makes their facial features easier to identify. The full-body view shows their outfit, silhouette and proportions.

Save the version you are happy with at its original available quality. Keep the original file rather than relying on a screenshot with interface elements around it.

Give it a name you can recognize, such as:

Amina — Main Character Reference

You can create additional views later, but begin with one clear reference.

Using the same reference helps guide consistency; it does not guarantee that every generated frame will match perfectly. You still need to review each result.

Also, consistency does not mean the character must wear the same outfit forever. Their identity should remain recognizable even when their clothes or surroundings change.

5. Choose your first video idea

For your first attempt, keep the idea simple:

One main character. One clear action. One short scene.

Choose a video you created, have permission to adapt, or are otherwise allowed to use. A clip being publicly available does not automatically give you permission to republish a modified version.

Look for a reference with:

  • A main subject you can identify easily.
  • A face or body that remains reasonably visible.
  • Clear movement.
  • Limited obstruction from other people or objects.
  • A short action you can describe in one sentence.

For example:

A woman walks into a room, notices something unexpected and turns toward the camera.

That is a useful first exercise because you can check whether your character follows the walk, reaction and turn.

A crowded scene with several people crossing in front of each other gives you more things to manage. Save that for when you are comfortable with the basic workflow.

Give viewers a reason to watch

Before you remix, answer four questions:

  1. What will someone notice first?
  2. What happens during the clip?
  3. What makes them curious about the ending?
  4. What is the final reaction or payoff?

You do not need to make every scene outrageous. A clear reaction, a funny contrast or a satisfying transformation can be enough to give the video a point.

6. Open Scene Remix and add your references

Inside Fuse Atelier, open Scene Remix.

Upload your source video, then add the character sheet you saved.

Keep track of which attachment contains which reference. If you add several images later, label them clearly in your instructions.

For this first exercise, you only need:

  • Source video: The movement, camera work and scene to follow.
  • Character sheet: The person who should appear in that scene.

The video tells the model what happens. The character sheet tells it who should appear.

That distinction will help you write a clearer prompt.

7. Explain who should be replaced

In “Who should we replace?”, describe the subject in the source clip.

Use details that distinguish that person from anyone else.

For example:

Replace only the woman in the white blouse standing closest to the camera with the woman from my uploaded character sheet. Keep everyone else unchanged.

For a clip with one person:

Replace the only person in the source video with the character from my uploaded character sheet.

For a clip with two people:

Replace the woman on the left wearing the red dress. Keep the man on the right unchanged.

Avoid instructions such as “replace her” when more than one person could fit that description.

The clearer your target, the easier it is to judge whether the result followed your request.

8. Write the Scene Remix prompt

Your prompt should explain both the replacement and what you want preserved.

Start with this template, then adapt it to your clip:

Replace only the identified main subject in the source video with the adult character from my uploaded character sheet. Use the sheet as the visual reference for their face, skin tone, outfit and body proportions.

Follow the source subject’s actions, expressions, gestures, movement and timing. Preserve the original camera movement, framing, shot order, background and placement of other people.

Keep the replacement character naturally positioned within the scene. Match the lighting, shadows, perspective and scale. Maintain believable hands and contact with objects or other people.

Keep the character’s identity consistent throughout the clip. Avoid duplicate subjects, extra limbs, face blending, sudden outfit changes and unintended changes to other people.

Add one sentence describing the action in your particular video:

The character should walk forward, stop at the same point, react and turn toward the camera in the same sequence as the source subject.

These are instructions for the generation, not a promise that every detail will be preserved perfectly. Review the finished clip before using it.

If source audio matters to the finished video, check it too. Make sure you have permission to use any audio you publish.

9. Generate and inspect the result

Review the selected settings and credit cost, then submit the remix.

Once it finishes, watch the whole video. A strong opening frame can hide problems that only appear during movement.

Check these areas:

Identity

Does the character still look like the person in your character sheet?

Watch for changes when they turn, smile, move quickly or pass behind something.

Movement

Does the action follow the intended sequence?

Check the walk, reaction, turn and ending—not just whether the character moves.

Hands and contact

If someone holds an object, carries something or touches another person, inspect those moments carefully.

Look for floating objects, incorrect grips or limbs passing through surfaces.

Outfit and background

Does the outfit remain consistent? Were other people or objects changed unintentionally?

If the result needs another attempt, identify the specific problem before spending more credits.

For example:

Keep the current scene and movement. Correct the character’s face during the turn so it stays consistent with the uploaded reference. Preserve the scarf and outfit throughout.

A focused correction gives you a clearer experiment than rewriting the entire idea without knowing what failed.

10. Prepare your first Instagram Reel or TikTok video

Choose the strongest part of your result.

Trim unnecessary waiting at the beginning. Start where the action, expression or visual idea becomes clear.

If you add on-screen text, keep it readable and relevant to what actually happens.

Examples:

POV: your AI influencer finally gets her first invitation.

I created the character. Then I put her inside this scene.

The same character. A completely different situation.

Your caption can briefly explain the idea or invite a response:

Meet Amina, my AI-created fashion character. This is her first scene—what situation should I put her in next?

Make it clear that the character or content is AI-generated. Use the applicable disclosure tools when publishing, and check each platform’s current requirements.

You can introduce a fictional personality without presenting that character as a real person.

11. Build a recognizable Instagram and TikTok presence

Your first video is a starting point. The next step is to give the character a consistent home.

Use:

  • A name people can remember.
  • A recognizable profile image.
  • A short bio explaining the character and content.
  • A clear indication that the character is virtual or AI-created.
  • A consistent tone in captions and replies.

For our example:

Amina | Virtual Fashion Creator
Modest style. Unexpected situations.
An AI-created character by [your name or studio].

Keep the character’s identity consistent across Instagram and TikTok so someone who finds both pages understands that they belong together.

Choose three repeatable content themes

Amina’s themes might be:

Style: Outfit reveals and fashion moments.

Personality: Reactions, humour and relatable situations.

Story: Short scenes that bring her into a recognizable fictional world.

You can also share how you created the work. A character sheet beside a finished remix can help people understand the process.

A manageable first set of posts could be:

  1. Introduce the character.
  2. Share the first remix.
  3. Show the character sheet and a short process breakdown.
  4. Create a second situation using the same character.
  5. Repeat the strongest idea with one deliberate variation.

Start with a pace you can sustain within your time and generation budget.

12. Learn from the response

After publishing, look beyond the view count.

Use the information available in your account to ask:

  • Did viewers stay to see the ending?
  • Did they share or save the post?
  • Did they visit the profile?
  • Did they follow?
  • What did they comment on?
  • Did anyone ask how the video was made?

Look for patterns across several posts. One successful or unsuccessful video is not enough to explain everything about your audience.

If people respond to the character’s reactions, explore more reaction scenes. If they enjoy the outfit transformations, develop that into a repeatable series.

Keep the recognizable parts and change one element at a time. That makes it easier to understand what viewers are responding to.

13. Understand how this skill can become an offer

Creating an AI influencer does not automatically produce income.

What you are building is a combination of creative skills: character development, visual consistency, scene direction and short-video production.

You could develop those skills into:

Character creation

Create an original virtual character and a reference sheet for a business or creator.

Short video production

Produce approved videos featuring a client’s character, product or campaign idea.

Brand content

Develop fashion, beauty, lifestyle or product concepts that fit a brand’s audience.

Your own media page

Build an audience around a character and explore suitable commercial opportunities as the page develops.

You do not need a large following to begin practising client-style work. You do need examples that show what you can deliver.

If you make an independent concept featuring a real product, label it as a concept. Do not imply that the brand commissioned or approved it.

Before accepting paid work, agree on the deliverables, usage, deadline, revisions and price. Brand interest and earnings depend on the quality of your work, its relevance and how you present the offer.

Your first assignment

Complete these five tasks:

  1. Write your character brief.
  2. Generate and save one character sheet.
  3. Choose a suitable source clip you are allowed to adapt.
  4. Create one video using Scene Remix.
  5. Review the result and prepare it for Instagram or TikTok.

You have completed the exercise when you can show:

“This is my character, this is the reference sheet, and this is the video I created with them.”

If your first attempt needs improvement, write down what went wrong and what you would change next. That observation is part of learning the workflow.
