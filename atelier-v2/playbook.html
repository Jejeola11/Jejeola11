FIRST CLIENT PLAYBOOK
7-DAY IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

Turn your skill into your first client.

One useful skill. One clear offer. Relevant proof. A respectful way to start conversations with businesses that need your help.

Start Day 1 ↓
0% completeMark each day when your task is done.
YOUR ROADMAP

Seven days. One simple system.

Do one day at a time. The action task is the lesson.

DAY 01

Choose your lane

Outcome: one AI-assisted skill and one type of business you understand.

Start useful, not perfect.

You do not need to learn everything at once. Choose one skill you can practise consistently: AI flyers and designs, UGC videos, landing pages, product visuals or AI video content. Your first goal is to create one clear result a business can use.

A niche is simply a group of businesses with something in common. Starting with one makes your samples relevant and your messages personal.

DAY 02

Package an offer people understand

Outcome: a small, clear starter package and beginner price.

A business buys a result, not your skill.

Instead of “I do graphics,” say what you create, who it helps and why it matters. Keep your first offer small enough to deliver well. Your price should state the deliverables, timeline, revision limit and price.

I help[business type]get[useful result]with[specific deliverable]

DAY 03

Create proof before clients

Outcome: three niche-specific samples that show your thinking.

Proof is not a random gallery.

You do not have to wait for a paying client. Create concepts for real types of businesses in your niche and label them as concepts. Each sample should show that you understand a business problem - not only that you can make something beautiful.

For every sample, write: the problem, what you created, and where the business could use it.

DAY 04

Make a simple portfolio

Outcome: one easy link a prospect can understand in one minute.

Keep it focused and easy to open.

Your portfolio can be a clean folder, a simple page, a PDF or a social-media highlight. Put your strongest sample first. Include your name, what you do, who you help, three samples and a way to contact you.

Your portfolio is not meant to impress everybody. It should help the right business think: “This person may be able to help me.”

YOUR ACTION TASK

Build your portfolio and open the link on another phone or in an incognito browser.

DAY 05

Find businesses worth messaging

Outcome: ten researched businesses and five strong fits.

Research first. Do not message everybody.

Look for businesses in your niche that already care about attracting customers and have a visible reason your service may help: a new service, promotion, event, weak visual, confusing page or active content.

For each business, record the name, link, what you noticed and the best public contact route.

YOUR ACTION TASK

Research ten businesses. Choose the five strongest fits for your offer.

DAY 06

Start respectful conversations

Outcome: five personal ask-first messages sent.

Your first message earns permission.

Keep it short, personal and easy to answer. Mention one real observation, share one useful idea, then ask if they would like to see it. Do not send a long biography or an unsolicited full sample.

Hi [name], I came across [business] and noticed [specific observation]. I had an idea for [specific improvement] that could help with [simple result]. Would you be open to seeing the concept?

DAY 07

Turn replies into paid work

Outcome: a calm next-step system for no reply, yes, sample, offer and delivery.

One conversation at a time.

People miss messages. Follow up after two or three days, then send one final close-the-loop message if needed. When someone says yes, thank them and ask what they are promoting, their audience, the desired action and their deadline - before you create anything.
